### 1. What is PBL?

- Includes integration of language skills for task completion.
- Involves refining + honing skills through completion of projects in AND outside of classroom
- Moves away from teacher-centered -> engages through inquiry and promotes development of critical of thinking skills
- in the field of English language instruction: means for students to improve their language + critical thinking skills in tandem
- Tricia Hedge first introduced as a means of development for English language learners (ELLs)

> A project is an extended task which usually integrates language skills through a number of activities. These activities combine in working towards an agreed goal and may include planning, the gathering of information through reading, listening, interviewing, etc., discussion of the information, problem solving, oral or written reporting, and display.
> (Hedge, 1993, p. 276)

### 3. How does PBL align with principles of CLT?

- Seven
- Seven characteristics CLT focuses on that help with understanding how PBL enhances ELL
  1. All aspects of language
  2. Function of language, form secondary
  3. Fluency > Accuracy
  4. Real-world contexts
  5. Language outside of the classroom
  6. Teacher as facilitator, guide
  7. Student-centered learning
- Integrates language skills -> all aspects are taught
- Often focus on IRL contexts
  - Example: Novel assignment -> Movie trailer as an assignment

### 4. What are the benefits of PBL in an English language classroom?

#### a. Group work

- Encouragement for further usage of English in authentic and meaningful contexts
- Development and growth through group work -> Increases language throughput

> “`[Group Work]`increases language practice opportunities, it
> improves the quality of student talk, it helps to individualize instruction, it promotes a positive affective climate, and it motivates students to learn...”
> -Ellis (2003)

- Less TTT, students get to use the language

#### b. Choices

- PBL is "exploratory in nature"
  - Outcomes depend on students' work ethic and individual choices
  - Requires students to take further ownership of their work, moreso than worksheets or textbook tasks.

#### c. Differentiated instruction

- Needs and feedback are better addressed while students workshop their projects

### 5. How is PBL similar to and different from Task-Based Learning (TBL) ?

> Bygate, Skehan and Swain (2001): “A task is an activity which requires learners to use language, with emphasis on meaning, to obtain an objective”

- PBL requires students to engage in _several_ tasks to complete a project >< TBL places emphasis on a single task

### 6. Possible challenges

- Students may struggle to work in a more open learning environment, as they may prefer a more traditional learning environment
  - For students who are more accustomed to teacher-centered, PBL must be taught in a classroom setting
  - Scaffolding may be helpful (Model -> Collab on smaller tasks -> Introducing a larger project)
- VERY dependent on student motivation
  - PBL's flexibility can be exploited for this front -> create more options for students (within parameters)

### 7. Suggestions for Implementing PBL

- **Be realistic - Compromise when needed**
- **Accept mistakes will happen**
- **Anticipate delays**
