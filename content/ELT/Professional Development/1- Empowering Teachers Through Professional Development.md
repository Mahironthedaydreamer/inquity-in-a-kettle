## I. Why professional development?

- To pursue professional development is to be empowered - have the opportunity and confidence to act upon one's ideas + influence the way one performs in their profession.
- Empowerment is the process through which teachers become capable of engaging in, sharing control of, and influencing events and institutions that affect their lives.
  - As teachers, we have the capacity to empower ourselves if we keep in mind the following precepts:
    - Be positive.
    - Believe in what you are doing and in yourself.
    - Be proactive, not reactive.
    - Be assertive, not aggressive.
  - Can also manifest leadership skills -> improvement in student performance + attitude
- Effective teacher requires a combination of professional knowledge + specialized skills as well as personal experiences and qualities.
- Ongoing PD is essential, especially in the world of today.
- Mounting challenges due to the very nature of school environments
  - Independent classes
  - School demands
  - Lack of feedback + supervision

## II. Find professional development activities that suit you:

- PD can take many forms:
  - Individual / informal / collective / structured, ...
- Most obvious form is through journal articles about teaching English, reading journals -> keep in touch
- Should be generally interactive and involve [[Reflective Teaching]]

## III. Possible techniques and activities:

### 1. Individual techniques

#### a. Teaching journal

- Observations + thoughts about teaching
- Able to examine why a lesson was successful and why one wasn't
- Describe, ask, formulate can reveal aspects that further a teacher's own PD
- Keeping a journal:
  - Private diary of sorts
  - Public discussion board for peers and self
  - Particular goal in mind

#### b. Incident analysis

- Look into an unexpected event during class
  - T writes down description of what occurred + why it happened and implications
  - **Critical event**: Something one had to pause to think about
- Suggested structure:
  - What happened before, how was it presented?
  - Results?
  - Contributing factors?
  - Reaction to incident?
  - How might things be changed after review?

### 2. Collaborative:

#### a. Shared journals

- Compare experiences and comment on solutions / teaching experience
- When writing to peers:
  - What is the group objective?
  - Why is it called "collaborative"?

#### b. Peer mentoring and coaching

- Useful for those who have not spent much time on the "other side of the desk"
- Can also motivate students to reflect + refine own strategies and techniques
- Both are collaborative endeavors that aim to:
  - Improve a particular aspect
  - Introduce policy and how to implement it within a classroom context
- Novice generally paired with experienced
- Goals include giving individualized attention, encouragement and strengthening teaching skills
- Not meant to be used to critique/ evaluate; instead:
  - Provides opportunity to focus on individuals
  - Support and share knowledge/expertise
  - Answer questions

#### c. Teacher support group/network

- Study circle adjacent
  - Regular meetings to discuss aspect/issue related to teaching
  - Agenda to follow, each teacher takes a turn to speak
  - **Strictly informal and collegial - no one is an "expert"**
  - A network usually includes teachers from several schools in the same community/region and has a more formal setting

#### d. Workshops and conferences

- Presenter usually someone knowledgeable about the subject being addressed
- Provide teachers with the opportunity to learn more about a specific area/skill related to teaching and learning within a set amount of time.
- Designed to give participants a chance to try out practical applications about the topic + contemplate how to utilize and adapt to their own classroom
- Excellent way to gain confidence and demonstrate their expertise
- Can provide teachers with information and strategies to create new policies or make changes in curriculum
- Enables teachers to develop long-lasting professional + personal contacts in the teaching community
