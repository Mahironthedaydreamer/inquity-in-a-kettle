## Preface

- Best carried out by a team
- Difficult to develop a successful test without the objective looks from peers, at what is being proposed at each stage of development
  - Clearly at stage of item writing, obvious faults are invisible to the writer themself
- Creative process
  -=>
- Willingness to accept justified criticism of the items they have written
- Other desirable qualities: Expert command, intelligence and imagination (context + foresee misinterpretations)

## Procedures:

1. Full and clear statement of the testing "problem"
   - Make oneself clear about: what is it one wants to know + what purpose
   - Significant questions:
     - What kind of test is it to be?
     - What is its purpose?
     - What abilites are to be tested?
     - Results: How detailed and accurate must they be?
     - How important is backwash?
     - What constraints are set by unavailability?
   - Carry out steps after identification of the problem
   - Efforts should additionally be made to gather information on tests that have been designed for similar situations, with samples if possible.
     - Not intended to copy other tests, rather to suggest possibilites and to help avoid "reinventing the wheel"
2. Writing specifications (Specs) for the test
   - Info on: Content, test structure, timing, medium, techs to be used, criterial levels of performance, and scoring procedures
     - Content:
       - Not to a single, particular version of a test, but to the entire potential content of any number of versions
       - Fuller info -> Less aribitrary on subsequent decisions
       - Overboard specifications may go beyond current understanding (prose reading)
     - Structure, timing, medium, techs
       - structure: What sections will the test have, and what will be tested in each?
       - Number of items (in total and in the various sections)
       - Number of passages (number of items associated with each)
       - Medium / channel
       - Timing: for each section + entire test
       - Techniques: What techs will be used to measure skills / sub-skills
     - Criterial levels of performance
       - Required level of performance for success
       - Can be more complex when it comes to language skills (speaking, writing)
     - Scoring procedures
       - Important, but particularly where scoring will be subjective
       - What scales will be used, how many will rate each piece of work, what happens if two or more disagree?
3. Writing and moderating items
   - Sampling
     - Unlikely that everything found under "content" can be covered by the items in one single version -> Choices will have to be made
     - Important to choose widely from the whole area of content, should not concentrate on elements known to be easy to test -> Wide and unpredictable
   - Writing
     - Written with specs in mind, no use writing "good" items without consistency
     - Look through the eyes of test takers + imagine how it may be misinterpreted
     - Should write more than specified
   - Moderating items
     - Scrutiny by at least two colleagues, neither being author of the examined items
     - Find weakness + remedy, reject if unable to be modififed
4. Trials of items on expert speakers
   - Presented to a number of expert speakers with general similarities
   - Difficult items and items with unexpected answers almost certainly require revision
5. Trials on non-expert speakers similar to intended audience
   - Surviving items are to be put together into the second test, under similar conditions with the official exam, with notes on administration and scoring problems
   - Usually unfeasible, due to lack of resources and security concerns
6. Analyze results -> commit necessary changes
   - Statistical + Qualitative analysis
     - Statistical: Reveal qualities of the test as a whole + individual items
     - Qualitative: Examine responses -> Discover misinterpretations, unanticipated but correct responses, any other indicators of faulty items
7. Calibrate scales
   - Dependent on oral or written exams
   - Collect samples of performance that cover full range of scales -> fine tune from experts
8. Carry out validation
   - Self-explanatory, essential for high-stakes or published tests
   - Lower stakes? Considered less necessary but still desirable for long-term use
9. Handbooks for test-takers, users and staff.
   - Should contain: Rationale, account of how it's developed, description, samples, advice, how test scores are intepreted, training mats, details, etc.
10. Training staff
11. Test maintenance
    - Statistical + Quality control should be regular -> Problem identification
    - Alternate versions may be necessary as word spreads of original content
      - Development cycle turns its wheel once more.
