## I. Pre - While - Post

### 1. Pre-stage

- Review and build student knowledge of relevant vocab, grammar points and background knowledge
- Crucial element of successful writing instructions
- Prepare learners for the writing task + activate subskills that prepare learner for completing the task.
- Typically focused on Ss, the content and the vocab necessary for the task, through word and phrase-level activities

### 2. While-stage

- Engage learners in recursive writing, editing and revision tasks
- T guides Ss through the process
  - Clear instruction and resources are required:
    - Drafts, revising, editing, expanding,...

### 3. Post-stage

- Written text is shared with others (as peer-editor, instructor or even with audience)
- Basic components:
  - Reread, add markers for a smooth flow
  - Eliminate "fluff"
  - Proofread for spelling, vocab, grammar
  - Edit and share

## III. Correcting Mistakes in a Writing Lesson

### 1. Traditional attitudes to mistakes and corrections

- "Mistake = bad"
  - S was being ignorant, lazy, deliberately rude to T for not paying attention / not learning the lesson carefully enough
- Instead of helping Ss, T would put crosses / "do it again"
  - Even if T did help, it would be provided in the correct model and getting them to copy it.
- A perfect writing lesson would be a flawless lesson.

### 2. The communicative approach to mistakes and corrections

- Mistakes are seen as positive steps towards learning
- Ss makes mistakes -> Shows the attempt to formulate sentences and express ideas for themselves and not simply copying T's model
  - They're learning by doing and will not make a perfect product the first time
- No mistake <=> No learning has taken place
  - Correction is seen as a technique to get Ss to refine what they want to say - an integral part of the lesson.
  - Positive attitude towards correction, techniques are used to **encourage** Ss, not to put them down or belittle them.
- A perfect writing lesson is a lesson full of Ss mistakes and Ss correcting themselves + each other.

### 3. Memory and correction

- Real learning takes place when SS are given the opportunity to internalize language and memorize it.
  - Often, rote repetition without understanding does not help internalization or memory.
- For this reason, it is better for the T to elicit the correction from the SS, not just write the correct answers in the SS’ books for them.
  - If the T elicits correction from the SS, by underlining the mistake and even coding what type it is, the S must think, discover their mistake, choose an alternative word or words and write it/ them again on their own in their books.
  - SS remember the correction for longer because their T has given them independence, encouragement and made them learn actively by getting them to write down their own corrections.

### 4. Ss ability to do self/peer correction

- A lot of Ts believe that SS are not able to correct themselves or each other and that the T must them a right model to copy.
  - This simply is not true.
  - Nearly all correctable errors are mechanical (accuracy) errors and as soon as the T points out to the S where the mistake in the sentence is, SS quickly and easily correct it for themselves.
  - If the mistake is a vocabulary mistake and SS cannot self-correct, again, in nearly every case, another S in the class can supply the new word.
- SS are happy to help each other and this sort of correction encourages SS to be independent and learn from each other instead.
  - In general, peer correction can be a time-saving and useful technique.
  - This does not release us from the duty of checking and evaluating S writing, but it can be a substitute for first draft reading. SS can work together on their first drafts, giving each other feedback on content, language and organization. They then rewrite and give the final version to the T.

### 5. What to correct and what NOT to correct

- Not necessary to correct every mistake in S's piece of writing.
  - Demotivating to see their passage covered in red ink
- Should give up using a red pen, and encourage them to express themselves and self-correct
  - Pencil!
- Should not correct every mistake - Should make a decision and select the most important things (target language / focus of the lesson)
  - Requires self-restraint and a systematic scheme for S's self-improvement.

### 6. Drafting + redrafting

- Rewriting very important
- Always be a first draft -> correction -> second draft -> ....
- Process:
  - First draft
  - Corrections, Ss self-correct
  - Ss copy out corrected piece as second draft
- T's job is to instill this process into Ss so it becomes a habit.
