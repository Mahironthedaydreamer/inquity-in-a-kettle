## I. Pre - While - Post

### 1. Pre-stage

- Prepares Ss by getting them to think about the topic or situation before they read, listen, speak or write about it.
- In other words, it gives SS a purpose to read, to listen, to speak or to write. It also gets SS to relate to what they already know about the topic.
- Activities:
  - Vocab tasks where the T pre-teaches vocab to aid comprehension and set the scene or explain the context.
  - Prediction tasks where SS guess what they are about to practice.
  - Brainstorming or discussion tasks where SS collect all their ideas on the topic.
  - In this stage, the T’s role is to get the SS thinking about what they are going to read/hear/talk or write about before they do it, so they are prepared and actively using their brains.
  - T has to think of and set up the pre-task, give clear instructions and monitor the task.

### 2. While-stage

- Gives Ss a guide or framework to help them practice the target skill of the lesson.
  - In other words, it helps Ss to do the skill better, more accurately, thoroughly.
- Activities:
  - Comprehension tasks where Ss answer questions based on the text.
  - Ordering tasks where Ss show that they have understood by putting pictures or statements into the same sequence as the text.
  - Transformation tasks where Ss take one format and transfer it to another.
    - For example, instead of simply telling Ss to write a paragraph about their own school, the T gives them a passage on Mai’s school and tells them to rewrite the information according to their school.
    - In this way, the T gives the Ss a framework which they can hang new information on. This kind of framework reduces mistakes, provides the correct vocab, grammar and sentence type.
- Also externalizes the process, so the T can check, through the tasks, if the Ss have understood. The tasks in this stage are both “teaching aids” and “diagnostic tests”.
- In this stage, apart from setting up the activity, the T doesn’t have to do a lot of teaching because the Ss work on the tasks by themselves, working individually or in pairs/groups.
- Instead, the T has to do a lot of monitoring and assist weaker Ss who have difficulty completing the task.

### 3. Post-stage

- Similar to **follow-up stage**. After SS have practiced the target skill in the While-stage, they do an extension activity. This helps SS take the information or whatever they have produced in the While-stage, and do something meaningful with it. This stage is usually an “information transfer” production type-exercise where SS respond to what they have just learnt.
- Activities:
  - For Listening and reading, the post task usually involves the productive skills (speaking or writing).
  - For Speaking, the post task usually involves writing it up. For example, if the speaking task is conducting a questionnaire for their friends, the post task could be writing up the results in a short paragraph. It may also involve an indirect correction session where the teacher gives feedback on what SS have said.
  - For writing, the post task usually involves correcting and rewriting.
- In this stage, the T once again gives instructions, monitors and evaluates SS’ performance.
