## II. Writing a lesson plan:

### 1. Why use a lesson plan?

- Preparation time for the teacher (T)
  - Baseline for the materials during a lesson, as well as the structure for the teacher's adaptation of such materials
    - Highlights focal points during a lesson, like what the students (Ss) must know after the lesson.
- A good way for T to manage time allocated for each section/activity.
- More importantly, it answers questions for T such as:
  - Where to go during a lesson and how to get there
  - What to use during a lesson and whether or not T and Ss are on the right track to it
  - How to know if the destination has been reached
- A good lesson plan provides T with a clear direction
  - The timetable and the roadmap of a lesson
  - Indicates the content of the lesson and helps to ensure a logical order of information, showing the learning techniques to be used + training aids needed when required
  - Logical providing of info -> Helps students understand + retain information
- Allows for a source of reference, highlighting where to pick up if deviation occurred + recommended periods of time for each part of a lesson.

### 2. What should an LP include?

- Class/Ss background
- Goals, anticipated problems
- Classroom arrangement for the lesson
- Materials, techniques, teaching aids
- Activites, work, timing for each activity, reserved activities
- Homework

### 3. Seven steps of writing a lesson plan:

#### Step 1: Check prior knowledge

- Baseline information such as class abilities, student behavior, etc,...

#### Step 2: Define objectives

- Only chosen after full consideration of aims + objectives of training, should address:
  - What students will learn (Learning)
  - How the students can apply learning in their day-to-day communication (Action)
  - **! Involve the use of [[Bloom Taxonomy]] / [[S.M.A.R.T Principle]]**

#### Step 3: Prioritize information

- Split the lesson into:
  - **Essential information** - what Ss **must** know
  - **Related information** - what Ss **should** know
  - **Complementary information** - what Ss **could** know
- \=> Ensure the information that is essential (must) is covered, understood and remembered. Then, include the remainder of information (should and could) depending on relevance, competence and available time.
- Better to deliver the most important information, presented in a simple form than covering as much info as possible in the time.
  - Too much detail -> confuses essential information with other less relevant material.
- "What is the best way to **LEARN** this topic?"

#### Step 4: Arrange the order of content

- Move from what is known to what is unknown
  - Baseline knowledge -> linking to new information presented in small, easily-understood blocks
  - When the unknown becomes known (Ss learned the info), move to the next unknown

#### Step 5: Selecting resources

- Basic resources (training room, audio-visual aids, handouts, etc..) should be picked according to course content, Ss, availability
- When resources are decided -> test prior to lesson to check relation to lesson + familiarize with use
- Backups are highly advised in case of mishaps

#### Step 6: Selecting teaching techniques

- Decide the most appropriate techniques for demonstration
  - Dependent on age, competences, Ss count, etc...
- These techniques include:
  - **Brainstorming**: Everyone asked to contribute ideas to write up on a board, paper without comment - evaluation is only done after all ideas have been written down. => More variation and quantity compared to solo work
  - **Q\&A**: Asking questions help to check understanding, encouraging to ask questions allows to clear misunderstandings + helpful to involve group response.
  - **Problem-solving exercises**: Promote teamwork through development of creative, supportive, innovative approaches to a certain problem
  - **Case studies**: Specific example reviewed for discussion + comment (may be based on a real situation or represent a combination of real cases based on the teacher's experience)
  - **Roleplays**: Ss act in a representation of a situation - can help change attitudes and learn how others think and feel (get into character). Need to be well-planned and facilitated, requiring a series of steps to explain + allocate roles + transfer experience into real world.
  - **Quizzes, debates, games,...**:
    - Education level and experience should be taken into account
    - Technique variety is advised
    - Different audio-visual materials can ensure concentration - provided there are enough copies and activity sheets and the material is relevant.

#### Step 7: Estimating segment times

- How much time is needed for each segment of the lesson?
  - Too much -> Cut down
  - Too little -> Extend
- Prioritize activities according to **Must - Should - Could**

## III. Principles of a lesson plan:

### **Variety** and **Flexibility**

#### Variety:

- Involving students in a number of activities of different types + introducing them to a wide selection of materials
- Planning so learning is interesting and never monotonous for Ss

#### Flexibility:

- Comes into play when dealing with the plan in the classroom - maybe when the provided material is not appropriate for the class on that day
- Ability to change plans if such an occasion arises -> Expected from an adaptable teacher

## IV. Why?

- Dangers of routine and monotony and how Ss may become demotivated if always facing the same type of classes
- Can only be avoided if T genuinely believes the learning experience should be permanently stimulating and interesting.
  - Difficult to achieve, but possible with variety (Adapt to styles + Different approaches)
- Aim must be to provide a variety of different learning activities -> helping students to get to grips with the language -> Giving students a purpose and telling them what it is
  - Ss must know why they are doing something + what is it that they will achieve
- A good lesson plan involves mixing techniques, activities and materials for an ideal balance during the lesson.
  - **Help them answer the question: "What can I do that I couldn't?"**
