## I. "PPP"

- A lesson is normally divided into three parts: **Presentation, Practice, and Production.**

### 1. Presentation

- New language is presented
  - Can be vocabulary, new grammar, a listening task, a listening activity, ...
  - One common example: T extracts model sentences from a dialogue and explains the grammar.
- Teacher does the main work during this part.

### 2. Practice

- Students are made to practise new language with T
- Target language is isolated
  - T provides practice in a Controlled Environment, with great emphasis on **ACCURACY**
  - Practice activities include: drills, pair work, assignments, etc...
- Necessary because students need to
  - Memorize target forms
  - Say the target language to a desirable level
- T spends time correcting errors
- Teacher and Students share the amount of work during this part.

### 3. Production

- Ss are to "produce" the new language with little interference from the teacher.
  - Usually through a speaking activity (discussion, games, roleplay)
- Ss must utilize productive skills (Writing, Speaking)
  - Add the new grammar / vocabulary to what they already know.
  - Ss learn to speak by themselves for _fluency_
- Not controlled by the teacher - sometimes called the Free Practice stage
- Students do the main work during this part.

## II. Starting and Finishing

- T can use a "warm-up" to start: A five-minute activity to wake up students and put them in the mood for learning, should include physical movement.
- Homework at the end of the stage: Either an extension of **Practice** or **Production**
  - Written exercise -> Extension of **Practice** as it is for accuracy
  - Free writing -> Extension for **Production** because it is for fluency

## III. Correction

- During **Presentation**, correction is not needed (0%)
- Extensive and thorough corrections during **Practice** (80-90%)
- No direct correction during **Production** (30-40%)
  - Tampers fluency
  - Indirect correction is advised instead, through notes or at the end of the lesson.

## IV. Timing

- Assuming the lesson is 45 minutes:
  - **Practice: 20 minutes**
  - **Production: 15 minutes**
  - **Presentation: 10 minutes**
    - Teachers usually spend too much time into this section because they believe their job is to make students **UNDERSTAND** English but this is only part of their job
    - Students need to also be able to **PRODUCE** English as well.

## V. Pacing

- Sometimes the allocated time is not enough -> Split content into 2 lessons/sessions
  - Lesson 1: **Presentation - Practice** -> Lesson 2: **Practice - Production**
- Smaller scale PPP

### "TTT"

- **Teacher Talking Time.**
- **80% - 40% - 5%**

## VI. Deep - End Approach and Other Alternatives

### 1. Deep - End Approach:

- PPP came under questioning in the 90s
  - Too teacher-centered
  - Students are assumed to learn in "straight lines"
- New approach came to light:
  - Starts with **Production**: What do students already know?
  - Supplies prior knowledge with **Presentation**
  - **Practice** after **Presentation**, then loop back to **Production**

### 2. Other Alternatives

- **Test - Teach - Test**
  - Prior knowledge tested -> New knowledge supplied -> New knowledge tested
- **Authentic - Restricted - Clarification -> ARC**
- **Observe - Hypothesize - Experiment -> OHE**
- **et cetera, et cetera...**
