## I. Vocabulary - What needs to be taught?

### 1. Form: Pronunciation and spelling

- What a word sounds like + what it looks like
- Fairly obvious characteristics, one or the other will be perceived when encountered for the first time
- Need to be sure both are accurately presented + learned

### 2. Grammar

- Needs to be taught if not covered by general grammatical rules
- An item may have unpredictable change of form / have some idiosyncratic way of connecting with other words
- Important to provide with this information at the same time we teach the base form
  - New verb -> past form, irregularity, transitive/intransitive.
  - New noun -> plural form, or lack thereof

### 3. Collocation

- The collocation typical of particular items are another factor that makes a particular combination sound “right” or “wrong” in a given context.
- Another piece of information worthwhile to teach.
  - When introducing words such as _decision_ or _conclusion_, may note the collocations such as _make a decision_ or _reach a conclusion_

### 4. Aspects of meaning:

#### a) denotation, connotation, appropriateness/formality

- Denotation:  Primary meaning in the real world
- Connotation: Association, positive/negative feelings evoked from the word
- Appropriateness/Formality: Whether this word should be used in a certain context or not.

#### b) Meaning relationships

- Synonyms: Same meaning
- Antonyms: Opposite meaning
- Hyponyms: specific examples of a general concept
- Super-ordinates: general concepts that cover specific items
- Translation: mother-tongued words equivalent in meaning
  \=> Can be exploited to clarify the meaning of a new item or for practice/test materials

### 5. Word formation

- Vocabulary items can often be broken down into component "bits"
  - How they're put together is another piece of info for more advanced learners
- Common prefixes may be taught - associations may not be constant
- Combining two words into a compound word, sometimes two seperate words

## II. PPP

### 1. Presentation

#### a) The Steps

- Introduce the item alone clearly, slowly 2 or 3 times for SS to perceive
- Say it in some words
- Contrast it with some similar one in the target language or in the mother tongue (minimal pairs)
- Write the word on the board
- Explain how to make the item visually or verbally
- Get SS to repeat it in chorus 2 or 3 times
- Get individual SS to repeat it

#### b) Techniques

| Technique             | Explanation                                                            |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Visuals               | Visual depiction                                                       |
| Mime                  | Act-it-out                                                             |
| Realia                | Real objects in the classroom                                          |
| Situation/Explanation | A situation is presented as an example of where the vocabulary is used |
| Example               | An example of the vocabulary being used                                |
| Synonym/Antonym       | Similar/Different meanings of the vocabulary                           |
| Translation           | The translation of the word into the learner's mother tongue           |

### 2. Practice

#### a) The Steps

- Give recognition drills
- Give SS imitation drills of the target item first in words
  - Then in groups of words or phrases
  - Then in minimal pairs
  - Then in sentences
  - Then in tongue twisters
  - Then in short dialogues
- Provide varied repetition (varied speed, volume, mood, etc.)
- Learn and perform dialogues
- Self-correction

#### b) Techniques

## III. Teaching Stress and Rhythm

**WAYS OF INDICATING POSITION OF STRESSES**

### 1. GESTURES

- T may indicate the stress pattern by
- Thumping the air when saying the stressed syllables
- Making a downward stroke of the hand like a conductor
- Punching the palm of his other hand
- Clapping his hands
- Striking a ruler on the board or table

### 2. THE BOARD

- T can use the board as the most valuable aid to make the stresses visible to SS in the initial **no-text stage** (when the written form of the language has not yet been introduced) and at the **text stage.**

#### a) Non-text stage

- Without writing the word or sentence, the T can indicate the stress pattern by using
  symbols
  Ex: O - o - O

#### b) Text stage

- T can give an example and write it in the above pattern on the board, for example:
  - _in ‘haler, good ‘morning_
- The SS are told to say out their own words or phrases to fit in tar pattern, for example:
  - _com ’biner, un ’written, in ‘order_
- T can write a sentence on the board then mark the stresses to practice its stresses and rhythm

### 3. TEACHER’S VOICE

- T can say the sentence, exaggerating the difference between stressed and unstressed syllables
- T can represent each syllable with a sound
  - _A sack of carrots -> deDAdeDAde_
- Ss immitate the rhythm, then say their own phrase to fit in the pattern
