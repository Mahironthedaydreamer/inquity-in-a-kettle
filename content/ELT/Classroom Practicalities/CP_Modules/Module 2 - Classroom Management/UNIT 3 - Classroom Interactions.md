## I. Definition of Classroom Interactions (CI)

- Patterns of communication and exchanges of meaning that occur between teachers and students and among students themselves during a lesson
- Includes:
  - T - S interaction
  - S - S interaction
  - Interaction with tasks and learning materials

## II. Possible problems in CI

- T-dominated interactions
- Unequal participation
- Low-quality or non-communicative interactions
- Affective and contextual constraints

## III. The balance of Teacher Talking Time (TTT) & Student Talking Time (STT)

- Effective language teaching aims to **maximize STT** and **minimize unnecessary TTT**, especially in communicative stages of the lesson
- Importance
  - Learners require opportunity to actively use target language
  - Excessive TTT limits chances to develop fluency and confidence
  - Meaningful student interaction supports cognitive and linguistic development
- Appropriate use of TT
  - Giving instructions
  - Modeling pronunciation / tasks
  - Providing feedback / scaffolding
    \=> Goal is not to eliminate TTT but to make teacher talk interactionally aware + pedagogically effective.
- Strats to increase STT
  - Pair, group work
  - Open-ended questions
  - Wait time
  - Elicitation techniques
  - Student-led communicative tasks

## IV. Work Arrangement

- Core concept: Interaction patterns
  - S - T communication in the classroom
- Goal:
  - Transition from Lockstep to Learner-centered interaction
- Contunuum of interaction:
  Whole Class -> Individual -> Pairwork -> Groupwork

## V. Factors of grouping students (Why)

- Strategic Grouping: Beyond "random" selection
- Why it matters: Classroom dynamics depend on how well students work together
- Criteria:
  - Proficiency level
  - Personalities
  - Friendship / Rapport
  - Task requirements
