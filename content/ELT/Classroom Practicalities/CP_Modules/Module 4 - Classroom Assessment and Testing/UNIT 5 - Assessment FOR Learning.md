## I. Effective Questioning Strategies

| Criteria            | Traditional Classroom Questioning                      | AfL-oriented classroom questioning                          |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purpose             | Check recal and confirm correct answers                | diagnose understanding + support formative assessment       |
| Question type       | Short, closed questions with a specific correct answer | Diagnostic and formative questions that probe understanding |
| Focus of assessment | Checks learning outcomes                               | Assesses learning during instructions                       |

### 1. Role

- **high-quality questioning allows:**
  - Analysis of pupil responses and their questions -> find out what they know, understand and do
  - Usage of questions to find out what pupils' specific misconceptions are -> target teaching more effectively

### 2. Characteristics of effective AfL questioning

#### a. Level of Questions

- refer to [[Bloom Taxonomy]]
- Often divided into two categories: lower-level and higher-level questions
  - **LLQ are appropriate for:**
    - Evaluation of student preparation and comprehension
    - Diagnosis of student strength and weaknesses
    - Review and summary of content
  - **HLQ are appropriate for:**
    - Encouragement, for deeper and more critical thinking
    - Discussion encouragement
    - Stimulation for students to seek out information themselves

> [!NOTE] NOTE:
>
> 1. Questioning is **flexible and responsive**, not fixed at one rigid level.
>    Instructors use both lower and higher level questions
>    Teachers adjust question levels based on student responses
> 2. It's not necessary for precise classification of each question.
> 3. The key is whether or not the question serves the instructor's instructional purpose

#### b. Focus

- **From "What" to "How"**
  - Is this correct -> Why is this...
  - What is the answer -> How do you know this is...
  - Correctness -> Logic and Evidence

#### c. Open and Closed Questions

- Open-ended:
  - Several acceptable answers
  - E.g: How do you feel today?
- Closed:
  - Only a limited number of acceptable answers
  - E.g: What is the past tense of "go"?

|            | Open-ended question                                                                 | Closed question                                                                                                 |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Low-level  | What are some adjectives to describe a good friend?                                 | Is the noun 'information' countable or uncountable?                                                             |
| High-level | In your opinion, how will technology change the way we learn English in the future? | In the sentence “I have been knowing him for ten years”, which word makes the sentence grammatically incorrect? |

#### d. General and Directed Questions

| **Aspect**                     | General questions                                   | Directed questions                               |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Questioner**                 | The entire class                                    | A specific student                               |
| **Respondent**                 | A volunteer chosen from those who raise their hands | The named or selected student                    |
| **Purpose**                    | To check overall class understanding                | To assess or engage an individual student        |
| **Indicator of understanding** | Number of students volunteering to answer           | Quality of the individual student’s response     |
| **Instructor response**        | Revisit the topic if few students respond           | Adjust support or challenge based on the student |
| **Student participation**      | Encourages voluntary participation                  | Ensures participation from specific students     |
| **Cognitive demand**           | Often used for general checks                       | Can be used to challenge students beyond recall  |

#### e. Maximizing Student Participation

- Randomly select students to respond
- Close eyes on dominating students
- Minimize usage of "Are there any questions?"
- Nonverbal reactions should complement verbal responses.

##### f. Questioning as a tool for instructional adjustment

- Questioning is not an end in itself
  - Enables real-time instructional adaptation
  - Links questioning directly to responsive teaching
  - Reinforces the formative purpose of assessment
- Evidence generated through student responses should be used to:
  - Adjust teaching pace
  - Revisit key concepts
  - Provide targeted scaffolding
  - Modify subsequent learning activities

### 3. Designing effective questions

- Clarify learning objectives
  - Align questions with intended learning outcomes
- Target the right cognitive level
  - [[Bloom Taxonomy]]
- Use varied question strategies
  - Explain causes or reasons
  - Justify MCQ answers
  - Compare and contrast ideas or causes
  - Explain procedures or processes -> predict outcomes using reasoning
- Filter your questions
  - Activate prior knowledge
  - Key concepts
  - Promote peer discussion
  - Clear, unambiguous

### 4. Incorporating effective questions into your course

1. Alternative questioning formats
   1. T-P-S
   2. Small group discussions
   3. Online synchronous discussions
   4. Minute papers / low-stake writing
   5. Classroom polling
2. Student-generated questions
   1. Write questions before class
   2. Peer quizzing
   3. Exit questions at end of class
   4. Design discussion / exam questions
3. Positive questioning climate
   1. Wait time / silence
   2. Value incorrect answers as learning opportunities
   3. Probing, follow-up questions
   4. Encourage StS responses
   5. Connect student answers to lesson concepts
