## I. Classification of Assessment Purposes

### 1. Definition and Features

- Definition:
  - The end of a unit or grading period
  - Sum something up
- Features
  - Evaluate at the end of an instructional unit, against a certain standard or benchmark
  - High-value in points

## II. Comparison - OF and FOR

|                              |            Summative             |        Formative        |
| :--------------------------: | :------------------------------: | :---------------------: |
|     **Primary Purpose**      | Assigning grades, accountability | Adjusting instructions  |
| **Proximity to Instruction** |              After               |         During          |
|         **Feedback**         |       Infrequent, general        |   Frequent, detailed    |
|     **Impact / Stakes**      |               High               |           Low           |
|         **Function**         |      Assessment OF learning      | Assessment FOR learning |

## III. Assessment FOR Learning

### 1. Definition

> **“Assessment for learning (AfL) is part of everyday practice by students, teachers and peers that seeks, reflects upon and responds to information from dialogue, demonstration and observation in ways that enhance ongoing learning”**
> _- (Third Assessment for Learning Conference Participants, 2009)_

- Also known as Formative Assessment.
- Ongoing process embedded in every day classroom practice, involving collecting and responding to evidence from dialogue, observation and interaction to improve learning
- Helps teachers and learners:
  - Identify current learning levels
  - Clarify learning goals
  - Decide how to improve learning effectively
- AfL focuses on the PROCESS, not just final results.

### 2. Theory behind AfL

- Became prominent in the 1990s due to concerns about over-assessment
- Researched by Paul Black and Dylan WIlliam highlighted the importance of formative assessment.
- 1998: Published findings in an important booklet for practicioners - _Inside the Black Box: Raising Standards through Classroom Assessment_
- **Key findings**
  - F.A is at the heart of effective teaching
  - F.A has a strong positive effect on achievement
  - Improving F.A raises standards

### 3. Effective AfL

- Effective AfL:
  - shares learning goals with students,
  - helps students understand standards and success criteria
  - provides constructive feedback for improvement
  - assumes all learners can make progress
  - encourages teacher and learner reflection
  - develops self-assessment skills
  - enhances motivation and self-esteem.

### 4. Ten Principles of AfL

- Part of effective planning of teaching - learning
- Focus on how students learn
- Be recognized as central to classroom practice
- Be regarded as a key professional skill for teachers
- Be sensitive and constructive
- Take account of the importance of learner motivation
- Promote commitment to learning goals and a shared understanding of the criteria
- Ensure that learners receive constructive guidance
- Develop the learner’s capacity for self-assessment
- Recognise the full range of achievements of all learner

### 5. Benefits and Challenges of AfL

- **Benefits:**
  - Helps learners understand learning goals and standards of excellence
  - Feedback significantly improves achievement (Hattie, 2012)
  - Supports teachers in adapting instruction to learners’ needs.
  - Integrates teaching, learning, and assessment
- **Challenges:**
  - Confusing instruction with instrument
  - Fidelity of implementation
  - Assessing long-term recall
  - Change of practice + heavy workloads
