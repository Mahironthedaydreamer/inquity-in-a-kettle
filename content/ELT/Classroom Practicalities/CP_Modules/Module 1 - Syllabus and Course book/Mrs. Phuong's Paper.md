## I. Language ideologies

- In alignment?
  - Selective forms of knowledge and cultural value systems suited to authors' and publishers' interpretations of social reality
- Textbooks "not only mediate subject knowledge but also reproduce ideologies"
- Found books tend to support "native-speakerism" + western bias rather than globalization and multilingualism

### 1. Linguistic models

- Lack of EIL-informed approaches can be chalked up to the lack of instructional materials promoting English varieties
- Further noted that variations are limited in the design of textbooks for Vietnamese primary curriculum.
  - Strong preference towards Br-tish spelling, grammar and punctuation
  - Other models (Singaporean English, etc.) were overlooked
- Notably, localized linguistic features were scarce despite abundant reference to its local context
- Received Pronunciation[^1] was dominant, also heard in model conversations depicted to be between Vietnamese natives -> strong bias sensed.
  [^1]: Standard form of British English pronunciation, based on southern England's educated speech

### 2. English native speakers

- How they're depicted in relation to non-natives
- "Native-speakerism" (NS-ism)
  - NSs represent a "Western culture" -> Ideals of both English and English ELT
- In CBs, NS-ism was demonstrated from English teachers with Anglophone backgrounds were positioned as best models + language teachers in the 'linguistic market'
  - E.g.1: Reading text about digital English language learning -> use software tool **with assistance of 'different native English speakers'** ->  Prestige implied
  - E.g.2: Reading text about language course ad -> NSs associated with 'well-qualified', mainly based on speakerhood + 'Western origin'
    -> Cases of marginalizing + discriminating against teachers OUTSIDE the Anglophone circle
    -> Raised political concerns unknowingly propagated by these CBs.
