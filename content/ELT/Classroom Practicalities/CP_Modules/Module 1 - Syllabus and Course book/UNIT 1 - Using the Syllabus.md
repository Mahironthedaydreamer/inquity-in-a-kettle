## I. How are they different?

- Curriculum: Umbrella term
- Syllabus: Rough outline of the course
- Course book: Physical contents of the course

## II. An Introduction to syllabus design and evaluation

### 1. Syllabus: A Definition

- An expression of opinion on the nature of language + learning
- Acts as a guide for teacher + learner by providing goals to be attained
- Can also be seen as a "summary of the content to which learners will be exposed"
  - An approximation of what will be taught and cannot accurately predict what will be learnt.
- **Traditional definition:**

> At its simplest level a syllabus can be described as a statement of what is to be learnt. It reflects of language + linguistic performance.

### 2. Product-Oriented Syllabus

- Emphasize product of language learning + prone to authority intervention

#### a. Structural Approach

- Most prevalent is perhaps the grammatical syllabus
  - Selection + grading is based on complexity and simplicity of grammatical items
  - Learner expected to master each step and add it to their grammar collection
  - -> Focus on the outcomes of the product
- Ties connecting structural items may be feeble
- Syllabuses only focus on one aspect of language (grammar), while there are many more
- Divergence between grammar of the spoken and of the written language -> Implications for grading content.

#### b. Situational Approach

- Limitations led to an alt approach: point of departure became **situational needs** rather than **grammatical units**
- Principal organizing characteristic: a list of situations reflecting how language and behavior are used every day outside the classroom
- Linking theory to situations -> Learner is able to induce meaning from relevant context
- Motivation will be heightened since it is "learner-centered" instead of "subject-centered"
- However, will be limited for students whose needs were not encompassed by situations in the syllabus
- -> Led to Wilkins to describe notional + communicative categories (significant impact on syllabus design)

#### c. Functional / Notional Approach

- Structural and situational only answer **"how", "when"** and **"where"** of language.
- Instead, Wilkins enquires **"what it is they communicate through language?"**
- Thus, the starting point is the **communicative purpose + conceptual meaning** of a language (notions and functions), as opposed to **grammatical items** + **situational elements** (remain to a subsidiary degree)
- Learner needs to be analyzed by various types of communication the learner has to confront.
- Needs analysis has an association with notional- functional syllabuses.
  - Although implies a focus on learner, critics suggest a new list has replaced the old one, consisting notions + functions

> Language functions do not usually occur in isolation - White (1988:77)

- There are also difficulties of selecting and grading function + form

### 3. Process-Oriented Syllabus

- Also referred to as the analytical approach
- Developed as a result of a sense of failure in product-oriented courses -> enhance communicative language skills
- Process over product
- Focus isn't on what students will have accomplished, but rather what tasks and activities he/she will undertake during the course.

#### a. Procedural / Task-based Approach

- "what" becomes subordinate to "how"
- Focus shifts to pedagogical implications, emphasis on learning / learner.
- Ordering and grading content is no longer wholly significant for the syllabus designer.
- Arranging program around tasks (information + opinion-gap activities)
- Hoped to perceive language + concentrate on solving meaning behind tasks
- Boundary between this approach and language teaching methodology
  - Evaluating merits become complicated.
- Task-based approach assumes:
  - Speaking a language is a skill best perfected through practice + interaction
  - Uses tasks + activities to encourage learners to use the language with the intent to communicate purpose
- Tasks must be relevant to real-world language needs of a student
  - Underlying learning theory suggests activities that employ language is to complete meaningful tasks and enhance learning.

### 4. Learner-Led Syllabus

- Emphasis lays with learner
- Full awareness of the course they study
  - -> interest and motivation increase
  - -> positive effect of nurturing skills required to learn
- Predetermined syllabus should not be dismissed either
- Learner-led may seem "utopian", difficult to track as direction will lie upon students instead
- Lack of aims may arise without a course book

#### a. Proportional Approach

- Develop overall competence
- Theme as a link through the units - designated by learners
- Initially expected a central role, but veered towards interactive components later on
- Syllabus designed to be **dynamic**, not static.
- Shift from form -> interaction can occur at any time, not limited to a particular stratum of learner ability.
  - Indiate **what will be taught**, not "what will be learnt"
- Practical approach with focus on flexibility + spiral method of language sequencing into recycled language
  - Seems relevant for learners lacking exposure to target language beyond the classroom

- Teacher or learner-led?
- Goals and needs?
