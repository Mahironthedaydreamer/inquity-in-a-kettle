## I. Definition

- “The term Naturalism describes a type of literature that attempts to apply scientific principles of objectivity and detachment to its study of human beings.”
  - Oxford dictionary:
    - Objectivity: not being influenced by personal feelings or opinions but considering only facts
    - Detachment: not being involved itself in an emotional or personal way
- Through this objective of human beings, naturalistic writers believed that the laws behind the forces that govern human lives might be studied and understood.
- Center: the picture of an entrapped man against an indifferent world — a gloomy picture emphasizing the powers of environment, heredity, and circumstances.
  **Holman & Harmon’s A Handbook to Literature:**

> “Human beings are animals in a natural world, responding to environmental forces and internal stresses and drives, over none of which they have control and none of which they fully understand.”

- Man’s fate has been pre-determined and he cannot avoid it.

## II. Development

- Time period: the late 19th & early 20th centuries (approximately 1870–1920)
- Origin: Naturalism was first proposed and formulated by French novelist Émile Zola, and it was introduced to American novelists Frank Norris.
- Naturalism became the dominant literary movement in American fiction with famous writers:
  - [[Stephen Crane]], [[Frank Norris]], [[Jack London]] , [[Henry Adams]], [[O. Henry]], [[Ernest Hemingway]]…
    ⇒ Naturalism expands on the base begun by Realism, following the traits of Realism.
    “Basic” view of life and humanity, stripping away layers of Romanticism to present a “natural” or “real” outlook of the world
- Refuse to idealize or flatter the subject
- Avoid artificial, fantasy, or supernatural elements
  ⇒ Naturalism can be considered a more extreme form of realism.

## III. Social background

### 1. American Civil War (1861–1865)

- The Civil War was very violent and bloody. As a result of the brutality of the war, people decided to focus on reality rather than imagination, because fantasies became too improbable when compared to the harsh reality

### 2. Advance of Science

- Darwin’s theory of “Survival of the fittest”
  - In 1859, Charles Darwin set out his theory of evolution by natural selection. He defined natural selection as the “principle by which each slight variation, if useful, is preserved.”
  - The concept was simple but powerful: Individuals best adapted to their environments are more likely to survive and reproduce

## III. The Industrial Revolution

- The Industrial Revolution (1760–1840) was the transition to new manufacturing processes.
- Ordinary working people found increased opportunities for employment in the mills and factories, but these were often under strict working conditions with long hours of labour dominated by a pace set by machines.
- For workers of the labouring classes, industrial life “was a stony desert, which they had to make habitable by their own efforts.”
- The misery of the working classes in urban slums became one of the naturalists’ favourite themes in analyzing the human condition.

## IV. Characteristics of Naturalism

### 1. Characteristics

- Pessimism
- Determinism
- Darwinism “survival of the fittest”
- Surprising twist at the end

### 2. Characters

- Often poorly educated, lower class
- Controlled by forces such as heredity, animalistic instinct, low passion
- No free will or choice → Determinism
- Characters cannot control “the brute within”

### 3. Key themes

- According to Charles Child Walcutt’s American Literary Naturalism: A Divided Stream, there are 4 key themes:
  - “Struggle for survival”
  - “Determinism”
  - “Violence”
  - “Taboo”
- There are also other themes, which are:
  - The “brute within” individuals
  - The conflict in naturalistic novels often “man against nature” or “man against himself”
  - Struggle to remain civilized despite pressure to release “brute”
  - Free will as an illusion

### 4. Plots

- Often follow a “plot of decline”
  - → A plot that depicts progression toward degeneration or death, outcomes are shaped by social and natural forces, not personal choices.
  - Events unfold logically and realistically, often leading to tragic or inevitable endings.
  - Conflict arises from environmental pressures or social struggles rather than personal ambition or heroism.

### 5. Typical setting

- Settings are typically urban environments, industrial cities, slums, or rural areas affected by modernization.
- They reflect real-life conditions, often harsh and oppressive.
- The environment plays an active role in shaping characters’ lives and choices.

## V. Notable American Naturalists

### 1. [[Stephen Crane]]

- November 1, 1871 – June 5, 1900
- Journalist, poet, author of short stories and novels
- Crane is known for themes, a lyrical style, and vivid imagery in both poetry and prose
- Earned international fame for his novel [[The Red Badge of Courage]] at the age of 24
- His first book: “Maggie, a Girl of the Streets”, [[The Open Boat]]
  - → The Open Boat is one of his classic examples of naturalism
  - → A fictional account of a true experience

Born on September 11, 1862 (during the Civil War) in North Carolina
At the age of 20, he moved to Texas and started to write under the pen name “O. Henry.”
In 1896, he was accused of embezzling money from the bank, and 2 years later, he was sent to jail for 5 years

Writing style
He always created the special ends described as “tears with smile” for his stories.
Most stories are set in his own time, the early 20th century, and the background is often employed in New York City.
His stories showed his sympathy with the lower class.
