- Notable authors:
  - [[Chopin Kate]]
- An approach that attempts to describe life without idealization of romantic subjectivity
  - Although not limited to any century or writer groups, it is most often associated with the literary movement in 19th-century France - with French novelists Flaubert and Balzac
- George Eliot introduced realism into England, and William Dean Howells introduced it into the United States
- Attempts to describe life without any kinds of idealization or romantic notions - a down to earth, precise, unpainted representation of human life and its social conditions
- Chiefly concerned with commonplaces of everyday life among middle + lower classes, where character is a product of social factors + environment is the integral element in the dramatic complications
- Later writers + critics felt the excessive emphasis on external reality
  - Henry James turned to a psychological realism that closely examined the complex workings of the mind - at least, attempted to.
  - Realism in its extreme form puts too much emphasis on this - and too little on the inner qualities of the characters.
- Although it is merely a technique, it also denotes a particular kind of subject matter - representation of middle-class life.
  - Reaction against romanticism, an interest in scientific methods, systematizing of the study of documentary history, and the influence of rational philosophy.
- No clear distinction between realism and the related movement in late-19th century: naturalism.
  - "Realism" is difficult to define - in part because it is used differently in European contexts than in American literature.
  - To put too simply, one rough distinction made by critics: realism espousing(supporting) a deterministic philosophy + focusing on lower classes => naturalism.
- In American literature:
  - "Realism" encompasses the period from Civil War -> turn of the century
  - Post CW - increasing rates of democracy and literacy, industrialism and urbanization
    \=> Fertile environment for readers interested in understanding these shifts in culture.
