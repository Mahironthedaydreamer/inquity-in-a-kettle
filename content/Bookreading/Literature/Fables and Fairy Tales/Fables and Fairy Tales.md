# Fairy Tales

- Origin
  - Dates back to the 17th century - earlier perhaps?
  - Many based on French literature - ethereal creatures featured
  - Others are retelling of age-old tales - many were created by women and retold throughout history
  - Notable figures: Grimm Brothers, Charles Perrault, Hans Christian Anderson, [[Oscar Wilde]],...
- Characteristics
  - Usually a short narrative
  - Familiar stories - passed down generations
  - Follow organically from implied oral - popular traditions
    - The combination and recombination of familiar plots and characters, devices and images.
  - The scope made by language
    - Consists all of acts of imagination, conveyed in a symbolic Esperanto
    - Include certain kinds of characters and certain recurrent motifs
    - Communicates meaning through an imagery of strong contrasts and sensations
  - Usually a happy ending: Expressions of hope -> Releases from poverty, maltreatment, subjection -> place where wonders are commonplace + desires fulfilled.
- Elements
  - Special words
    - "Once upon a time... " "A long,  long time ago..."
    - "And they lived happily ever after"
      \=> Set in the past
  - Mythical characters
  - Characters embodying good and evil
  - Royalty
- Definition
  - A fairy tale is a short story containing fairies - magic as main ingredients
  - Found in nearly all civilizations
  - Designed to make kids learn about good + evil + moral values
- Popular fairy tales
  - Grimm's, 1001 Nights, Hans Christian Andersen's Fairy Tales

# Fables

- Origins
  - Western tradition effectively begins with Aesop, to him is attributed a collection of ancient Greek fables
  - Earliest known collection linked to Aesop fates to 4th century BCE
  - Flourished in the Middle Ages
  - Medieval fable gave rise to an expanded form: The beast epic (Lengthy episodic animal story replete with hero, villain, victim and an endless stream of heroic endeavour that parodied epic grandeur)
  - Zenith in 17th-century France in the work of Jean de La Fontaine, with the folly of human vanity as the theme
  - Found a new audience during the 19th century with the rise of children's literature
- Characteristics
  - Often use animals as main characters, presented with anthropomorphic characteristics (being able to speak, reason,..) => personification
  - Fictional
  - Short, no more than two or three characters.
  - Described through plants, animals, forces of nature and inanimate objects by giving them human attributes
    \=> Moral lessons, may be added at the end explicitly as a pithy maxim.
- Popular fables
  - The tortoise and the rabbit
  - The fox and the grape tree,..

# Comparisons

|               | Fables                                              | Fairy tales                                                                                            |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Definition    | Focuses mainly on giving important lessons          | Focuses mainly on creating a fantasy world to the reader                                               |
| Style         | Can be in prose/poem                                | Always in prose                                                                                        |
| Nature        | No heading meanings, meant to convey a moral lesson | Create a world of magic, leaving much room for imagination + fantasy, readers have to fill in the gaps |
| Good and evil | No conflict between good and evil                   | Always conflict between good and evil                                                                  |
| Characters    | Often animals                                       | FIlled with fairies, magical creatures                                                                 |
