## I. Background of the story

- A short story by Oscar Wilde published in 1888
- Tells the story of a young student in love, desperate to find a red rose to conquer the woman he loves.
- The story blends elements of Romanticism and Social critique, revealing the contrast between idealistic love and the harsh realities of the world.

## II. Summary of the story

- The tale began when a student regretted not having a red rose in his garden, essential for the woman he loved to agree to dance with him.
- A nightingale, hearing his regret, decided to look for the red flower for the student.
- The Nightingale sacrificed its life to create a red rose for the student who wanted to win the love.
- The bird sang all night with its heart pierced by a thorn, and the rose bloomed from its blood.
- But when the student gave the rose to the woman, she rejected it, preferring expensive gifts from another man.
- Disappointed, the student threw the rose away and decided that love was useless, returning to his studies.

## III. Characters of the story

- The Nightingale: This central character symbolizes sacrifice and aesthetics.
- The Student:
- He is a bookish and rigid person, selfish and calculating in love.
- The Beloved Woman:
- She is a beautiful girl
  - “She will dance so lightly that her feet will not touch the floor, and the courtiers in their gay dresses will throng around her”
- She is a utilitarian and materialistic person.
- The Rosebushes: These represent the student’s love for the woman. By the red rose journey, we can see the true romance value.

## IV. Themes of the story

### 1. Love

- The story shows the nature of love.
- The Nightingale believes in true, selfless love and sacrifices her life to create a red rose for the Student.

### 2. Materialism

- In “The Nightingale and the Rose,” Oscar Wilde shows the theme of materialism by contrasting true emotion with shallow values.
- The Girl is the most self-seeking as well as a possessive character in the story.
- The consequence is that rationality certainly creates materialism if it is not fortified with emotion.
- Her logic for refusing the Student’s red rose:
  - “ Everyone knows that jewels price is far more than flowers”

### 3. Intellectualism

- Intellectualism is showed in the Student, who treats love as a logical problem rather than a heartfelt experience.
- His rejection of the Nightingale’s sacrifice reveals the limits of cold reasoning in grasping the value of love.
  ⇒ Oscar Wilde criticizes Victorian society for valuing academic knowledge and logic more than sincere feelings

## V. Literary devices of the story

### 1. Poetic and Descriptive language

- Use of metaphors, similes, and carefully crafted rhythm.
- Lend an almost mystical quality that enhances the symbolic and emotive nature of the story.

### 2. Metaphor

- “Love is wiser than Philosophy, and mightier than Power.”
- “Death is a great mystery”

### 3. Foreshadowing

- Making predictions about what may happen later in the story; the ominous and looming death of the Nightingale .

### 4. Personification

- Giving human qualities to things and animals.
- Natural elements and concepts are also personified by attributing them human traits.

### 5. Irony and Satire

- Situational irony: The Nightingale dies for love, but the Student discards the rose.
- Dramatic irony: The reader knows The Nightingale’s sacrifice is in vain, but she does not.
- Verbal irony: The Student first praises love as supreme, then dismisses it as foolish.
- Satire: Criticize the frivolity and materialism of society, especially in the depiction of the student’s beloved and her superficial reaction to Nightingale’s sacrifice.

### 6. Symbolism

- Symbolism is key to understanding the underlying themes of the tale and Wilde’s critique of shallow society
  - The nightingale: symbolizes goodness, virtue and sacrifice
  - The rose: True love and true art, sacrifice and difficulty associated with love.
  - The girl: materialism and hypocrisy
  - The student: Cynicism as he can not appreciate beauty, naivety
