[[Emily Dickinson]]
`“Hope” is the thing with feathers -`\
`That perches in the soul -`\
`And sings the tune without the words -`\
`And never stops - at all -`

`And sweetest - in the Gale - is heard -`\
`And sore must be the storm -`\
`That could abash the little Bird`\
`That kept so many warm -`

`I’ve heard it in the chillest land -`\
`And on the strangest Sea -`\
`Yet - never - in Extremity,`\
`It asked a crumb - of me.`

## 1. Summary

- Emily Dickinson’s “Hope is the Thing with Feathers” compares hope to a bird that lives in the soul. It keeps singing its song even through storms and hardships, giving comfort and strength. No matter how difficult life becomes, hope never asks for anything in return.

## 2. Rhythming Scheme + Meter

- Rhythm: The steady, song-like rhythm mirrors the bird’s continuous singing.
- Form: The poem has three quatrains with an ABCB rhyme scheme.
- Meter:
- The poem follows common meter, a pattern often used in hymns.
- It alternates between iambic tetrameter (8 syllables) and iambic trimeter (6 syllables).

### 4. Themes

- **Hope as a powerful, enduring force within the human soul.**

> “Hope is the thing with feathers
> That perches in the soul,”

→ Emily Dickinson compares hope to a small bird that lives within the human soul, singing even in the face of adversity.

> “And sings the tune without the words,
> And never stops at all,”

→  the feeling of hope that exists deep inside us. It doesn’t need language to be understood, we can feel it even when it cannot be expressed.

- **Hope as resilience**

> “And sweetest in the gale is heard;
> And sore must be the storm…”

→ Even in the midst of “storms” and “gales,” the bird of hope continues to sing, symbolizing the steadfastness and resilience of the human spirit

> “I’ve heard it in the chillest land,
> And on the strangest sea.”

→ Hope is present everywhere, even in the most desolate or unfamiliar places, reflecting its universal and everlasting nature.

- **Hope as selfless and unconditional**

> “Yet, never, in extremity,
> It asked a crumb of me.”

→ Hope never demands anything in return. It simply exists to uplift and strengthen the human soul.

### 5. Literary devices

#### a, Metaphor:

- The metaphor of a bird represents “hope” showing that hope always lives within the human soul, singing even in times of hardship.

#### b, Imaginary:

- “gale”, “storm”, “chillest land”, “strangest sea” → These images symbolize the challenges and hardships of life.

#### c, Repetition

> “And sings the tune without the words,
>  And never stops at all,
> And sweetest in the gale is heard;
>  And sore must be the storm”
>  
> →   Emphasizes the endless, everlasting nature of hope. It never ceases to exist

#### d, Symbolism

- Bird: symbolizes hope – light, free, and limitless.
- Feathers: represent fragility yet strength – like hope, delicate but never destroyed.
- Storm / Sea: symbolize the trials and struggles of life.
