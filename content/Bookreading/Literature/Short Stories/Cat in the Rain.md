## I. Background

- "Cat in the Rain" is a short story by American author Ernest Hemingway (1899-1961), first published by Boni & Liveright in 1925 in the short story collection In Our Time.
- The story is about an American husband and wife on vacation in Italy. Critical attention focuses chiefly on its autobiographical elements and on Hemingway's "theory of omission" (iceberg theory).

## II. Summary

- Ernest Hemingway’s short story “Cat in the Rain” takes place in a small hotel in Italy, where an American couple is spending a rainy afternoon.
- From their hotel room, the wife sees a cat crouching outside in the rain, trying to stay dry under a table. Feeling sympathy for the animal, she decides to go outside and rescue it. Her husband, George, remains in bed reading, showing little interest in her actions or feelings.
- As the wife goes downstairs, she meets the hotel owner, an old and dignified man who treats her with warmth and respect.
- His politeness and attention make her feel valued - something she does not experience from her husband.
- She is accompanied by the maid to bring the cat in, but when she reaches the spot where she saw it, it has disappeared, leaving her disappointed and empty.
- Back in her room, the wife begins to voice her deeper dissatisfaction with life.
- She talks about wanting long hair, nice clothes, candles, and above all, a cat to hold and care for.
- Her desires symbolize her yearning for love, comfort, and stability, which are missing in her marriage. George, however, remains detached, absorbed in his book.
- In the end, the maid brings her a cat, likely sent by the kind hotel owner.
- The story closes ambiguously, emphasizing the wife’s emotional isolation and the contrast between her longing for affection and her husband’s indifference.

## III. Analyzing modernism in the story “Cat in the rain”.

### 1, Minimalist style

- Hemingway uses short, simple sentences and very few details.
- Emotions are not directly explained - readers must infer the deeper meaning.
- This reflects the “Iceberg Theory” of Modernist writing.

### 2, Alienation and loneliness

- The American wife feels emotionally distant from her husband.
- The empty hotel, the rain, and the quiet setting emphasize isolation and disconnection - common Modernist themes.

### 3, Symbolism

- The cat symbolizes the wife’s desire for love, comfort, and stability.
- The rain represents sadness and emotional repression.
- The hotel keeper symbolizes the affection and attention missing in her marriage.

### 4, Identity crisis

- The wife has no name - only “the American wife.”
  → This shows a loss of personal identity and the emptiness of modern life.

### 5, Open, ambiguous ending

- The maid brings a cat at the end, but we don’t know what it truly means.
- There is no clear resolution, leaving readers to interpret - a hallmark of Modernist fiction.
