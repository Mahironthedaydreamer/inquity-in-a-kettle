## I. Background

- "The Storm" is a short story by American writer Kate Chopin, written in 1898.
- It is set in the American South during the 19th century.
- The story was not published during Chopin's lifetime and first appeared in print in 1969. It is also a sequel to another of her stories, "At the 'Cadian Ball."

## II. Plot summary

- The story focuses on  Calixt, a woman who is at home alone when a violent thunderstorm begins. Her old lover, Alcée, happens to be riding by and asks to take shelter from the rain. While the storm rages outside, their old passion for each other reignites, and they have an affair.
- When the storm ends, Alcée leaves. Calixta's husband and son return home, and instead of being in a bad mood, she is incredibly happy and loving towards them. Alcée also feels happy and writes a kind letter to his wife.
- The story ends by saying, "So the storm passed and everyone was happy."

### III. Themes

- The Storm is a Symbol: The storm represents the powerful, wild, and natural feelings of passion inside the characters. As the storm gets stronger, so does their desire. When the storm passes, their passionate moment also ends.
- Desire is Natural: The story says that a woman's sexual desire is normal, not a sin. It even makes the main character happier.
- No Punishment, Only Happiness: Instead of punishment for the affair, everyone in the story is happy at the end. This was a shocking idea for its time.
