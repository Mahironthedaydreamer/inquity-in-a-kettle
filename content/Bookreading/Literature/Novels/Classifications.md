- Historical
  - Ready-made plots and characters
  - A charade (historical-adjacent)
    - Has a popular appeal
    - Use the trappings of story
    - No real assimilation of the past into imagination
- Picaresque
  - Connote as much the episodic nature of the original species as the dynamic of roguery
  - Requirements:
    - Apparent length, loosely-linked episodes almost complete in themselves, intrigue, fights, adventure and stories within the main narrative,...
- Gothic
  - Designation derived from architecture, carried connotations of rough and primitive grandeur
    - Dark, tempestuous atmosphere full of madness, outrage and revenge
    - Approached in a spirit of deliberate suspension of the normal canons of taste
      - Example: Mary Shelley's Frankenstein
  - Gothic fiction: ingenious entertainment, pity and terror
- Epistolary
  - A story is told through dialogue of the characters
  - In 20th century: the letter-writing art had fallen into desuetude -> the transcribed tape recording - a device for expressing alienation than a tool of dialectic (Beckett’s play Krapp’s Last Tape)
  - Similarity: the power of seeming to grant direct communication with a fictional character, with no apparent intervention on the part of the true authors
- Sentimental
  - Term coined in the mid-18th centuries, signified refined/elevated feelings
  - Exhibits a passionate attachment between the sexes, rising above the merely physical
  - Sentimental novels characterized by emotion and sorrow
- Romantic
  - Focus on relationships and romantic love
- Detective
  - Crimes
- Fantasy
  - Mysterious fiction
