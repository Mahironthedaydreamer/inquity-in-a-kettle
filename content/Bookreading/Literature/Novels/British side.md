- The 18th Century
  - 1719: The first English novel
  - Main characteristics: 18th century novels were labelled as "realistic novels"
    - Theme: Real problems and issues, related to novelists and a realistic approach to people
    - Characters: Real people with ordinary names + surnames
    - Settings: Real geographical places
    - Character development: Progressions in time
      \=> Met the demands of the Middle Class, who questioned personal identity, moral virtues and responsibilities
- The Romantic Period (1798-1837)
  - Theme:
    - The writer's emotions and imaginations
    - Beauty of nature
    - Rights and freedoms of an individual
    - Idealization of women
  - Style
    - Individual freedom
    - 1st person narrative
    - Use of common languages
- The Victorian Era (1837-1901)
  - First Generation Victorian Novel (40s-50s)
    - Theme: the specific contemporary problems of the Victorian society: satirists, humanists and moralists
    - Plots were formless for classes, serialized monthly on magazines
    - Readers and novelists share ideas + optimistic: trust the progress
  - Second Generation Victorian Novel (60s-70s)
    - Novels were more literary and less popular, more academic flavors and poetic imagination.
    - Theme:
      - Passions of life, particularly love
      - The rural England which was being destroyed by industry + commerce rather than the city working class
    - Shift towards intellectualism: The novelists were well-learnt.
- The 20th century: Modern novels
  - Theme:
    - Realistic in doubts, conflicts and frustrations of the modern world
    - Discussion of social problems + love for romance and adventure
    - Frank in sexual matters
  - Lack of a plot: Centered by the mental processes of the characters
    - Interior monologue constituting the main expressive means used by the writers
