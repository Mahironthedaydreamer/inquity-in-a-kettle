## I. The 18th century

(The flourishing of the novel )

- Main characteristics:
  - The 18th century novel was labelled as realistic novel
  - Themes: The real problems & issues relating to the novelists and giving them a realistic approach to people
  - Characters: The real people with ordinary names & surnames
  - Settings: The real geographical places
  - Character development: a progression in time
    → Meet the demands of the Middle Class, who questioned personal identity, moral virtue and social responsibility in reality.

## II. The Romantic Age  (1798 – 1837)

- Theme:
  - The writer’s emotions & imaginations
  - The beauty of nature
  - Rights and freedoms of an individual
  - The idealization of women
- Style:
  - Individual freedom
  - The 1st-person narrative
  - Use of common language

## III. The 1st Generation Victorian Novel (40s – 50s)

- Theme:
  - The specific contemporary problems of the Victorian society: satirists, humanists & moralists
  - The plots were formless for all classes, serialized monthly in magazines
  - Readers and novelists had the same ideas and were optimistic; they trusted in progress

## IV. The 2nd Generation Victorian Novel  (60s – 70s)

- Novels were more literary and less popular, having more academic flavours and more poetic imagination
- Theme:
  - Passions of life, particularly love
  - The rural England, which was being destroyed by industry and commerce rather than the city working class
  - The shift towards intellectualism: The novelists were well learned

## V. The 20th century  (The modern novel)

- Main characteristics
  - Theme:
    - Realistic in doubts, conflicts and frustrations of the modern world
    - Discussion of social problems and a love for romance and adventure
  - Frank in sexual matters
  - The lack of a plot: mainly the mental processes of the characters
  - The interior monologue which constitutes the main expressive means used by the writers
