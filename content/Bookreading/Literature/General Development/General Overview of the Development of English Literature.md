## I. Literature of the Middle Ages

### 1. Anglo-Saxon period (5-10th century)

- Epics: songs about the most
  remarkable events of history and
  heroic deeds.
- Anglo-Saxon poetry: no rhyme but
  alliteration in each line
- The epic poem: “The song of Beowulf”

### 2. Anglo-Norman period (11-13th century)

- Split into three hierarchies:
  - Ruling class:
    - Norman nobility with French as the primary language and romance as the main theme of literature
    - The clergy with Latin as the primary language and its literature themes being scholastic, moralizing and supportive of the feudal system
  - Serfs:
    - The common people with Anglo-Saxon as main language and [[Poetry]] as its main literature

### 3. Pre-Renaissance (14-15th century)

- The 14th century:
  - English as the spoken language
  - English literature was born
  - New spirit: Optimism
    - [[Geoffrey Chaucer]]
- The 15th century:
  - Folklore:
    - Ballads were written as quatrains, depicting historical and legendary figures, fictional or not.
    - Subjects:
    - [[Robin Hood]]

## II. Literature of the Renaissance

Core themes:

- Progessive ideology of humanism
  - [[Utopia]] - Thomas More
- English drama flourishing
  - [[Robert Green]], [[William Shakespeare]]

### 1. 15th-16th century:

- Rise of the progressive ideology: Humanism]]
  - Emphasis on the happiness of people belief in man's abilities and the equality of people
  - Strictly anti-feudal and anti-bourgeois

### 2. Mid 16th - Early 17th century:

- Flourishing of drama
- Playwrights: John Lyly, Robert Green, Ben Johnson, [[William Shakespeare]] - the most outstanding dramatist, playwright of all time.

## III. Literature of The Enlightenment (18th century)

### The Enlightenment...

- Development of Science and Culture
- Focuses on a central problem: Man and his Nature

### ...and The Enlighteners

- Defending common people's interests
- Against social inequality, religious hypocrisy and aristocracy's immorality
- Believing in reason, man's inherent goodness and powerful educational value of art
- Helping people see the roots of evil and social formation

## IV. Literature of The Romanticism

- Romanticism:
- The bringfulness of feeling, imagination, and passion.
- Literature:
  - Simple language
  - Lyrical
  - Describing everyday life
- Literary men:
  - Love of Nature, solitude, tranquility
  - Power of lyricism and individualism
  - Love of freedom

- More dramatic revival of [[Novels]] than [[Poetry]]
