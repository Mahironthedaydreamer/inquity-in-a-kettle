- Life:
  - Native of Saint Louis and spent ~14 years in Louisiana
    - Identified with the South
  - Married Oscar Chopin, a young cotton broker and moved to New Orleans, later Cloutierville near the Red River.
  - Died of a brain hemorrhage after a strenuous day at the Saint Louis World's Fair
- Writing style + career:
  - When she began to write in 1890, it was the Creoles and 'Cadians of her Louisana experiences that animated her fiction.
  - Distinctly unsentimental, relied of popular period motifs
    - Conflict of the Yankee businessman
    - The Creole, a theme that informs her first novel
  - These vivid and economical tales, richly flavored with local dialect, provide penetrating views of the heterogeneous culture of south Louisiana.
  - Many of them were collected in Bayou Folk (1894) and "A Night in Acadie".
  - Chopin's second novel, The Awakening(1899), also strongly evokes the region, but is primarily a lyrical, stunning study of a young woman whose deep personal discontents lead to adultery and suicide.
    - Praised for its craft and damned for its content, the novel was a scandal, and Chopin, always sensitive to her critics, gradually lost confidence in her gift and soon ceased to write.
