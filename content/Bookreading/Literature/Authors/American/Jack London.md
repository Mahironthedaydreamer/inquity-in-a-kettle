## I. Background

- 1876–1916
- Lived in the lowest part of society in his youth
- Decided to change his life by intellectual effort
- Join Klondike Gold Rush (1897) → Returned poor but inspired → transform harsh Arctic life into themes of survival & human endurance.
  ⇒ This combination of self-education, field experience, and philosophical inquiry gave London’s writing both intellectual depth and raw realism.

## II, Literary style

- Jack London’s literary style reflects his self-taught background: vigorous, direct, and naturalistic.
- Avoid ornate Victorian prose; prefer clear, vivid, and rhythmic sentences with strong verbs and sensory imagery.
- Write from real-life experience, not from scholarly distance — a participant, not just an observer.
- Focus on naturalism and survival — depict harsh realities of life in wilderness or frontier settings.
- Show human helplessness against nature’s deterministic forces (e.g., To Build a Fire).
- Offer unique perspectives, including animal viewpoints in The Call of the Wild and White Fang.
- Explore themes of atavism, adaptability, and the irresistible “Call of the Wild”

## III, Career

- Write over 50 books (fiction & nonfiction) in just 17 years.
- Become the highest-paid and most widely read author in the U.S. and worldwide.
- First success: The Son of the Wolf (1900).
- Breakthrough work: The Call of the Wild (1903) — brought international fame.
- Other major works: [[The Law of Life]] (1901),  The Sea-Wolf (1904), White Fang (1906), The Iron Heel (1908), Martin Eden (1909), John Barleycorn (1913).
- Pioneered adventure-naturalist fiction, blending realism, nature, and human endurance.
- Influence generations of writers and help shape modern adventure literature
