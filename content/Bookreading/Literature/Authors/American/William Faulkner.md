- ### Biography & Background
  - Born in New Albany, Mississippi
  - Grew up in Oxford, Mississippi, the model for his fictional Yoknapatawpha County
  - Influenced by his great-grandfather, a Civil War colonel and writer
  - Briefly served in the Royal Air Force during WWI
  - Awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature (1949) for his powerful and innovative prose
  - Served as Writer-in-Residence at the University of Virginia (1957–1958)
  - Home Rowan Oak is now a museum and literary landmark

- ### Writing Style & Themes
  - Known for experimental techniques and modernist narrative structures
  - Used stream of consciousness to portray characters’ inner thoughts and emotions
  - Narrative often features non-linear time, multiple narrators, and shifting perspectives
  - Blends past and present, reality and memory, requiring active reader engagement
  - Themes: Focuses on sex, class, race relations, and the decline of the Old South

- ### Contributions
  - Challenges traditional storytelling with fragmented timelines
  - Redefines how readers perceive truth and perspective in fiction
  - Major influence on modernist and postmodernist literature

- ### Major Works
  - The Sound and the Fury (1929) – innovative use of stream of consciousness
  - As I Lay Dying (1930) – multiple narrators and disjointed time
  - Absalom, Absalom! (1936) – complex Southern history and identity
