- 1830-1886
- A link between her era and the literary sensitivities of the turn of the century
- Life:
  - Born and spent her life in Amherst, Massachusetts
  - Found deep inspiration in the birds, animals, plants and the changing seasons of the New England countryside.
  - Latter part of her life as a recluse due to sensitivity and to make time for writing - additionally for homemaking for her Congress father
- Not widely read, but knew the Bible, works of [[William Shakespeare]], and classical mythology in great depth
- A literary figure of great solitude
- Created some of the greatest American poetry of the 19th century
- Writing style:
  - More modern and innovative than [[Walt Whitman]]'s
  - Concise, compact, combines concrete things with abstract ideas in an almost proverbial, compressed style.
  - Her best works mock sentimentality - even to the point of heresy.
  - Terrifying existential awareness.
  - Like [[Allan Poe]], she explores the dark + hidden part of the mind, dramatizing death and the grave - yet she also celebrated simple objects (bee, flower)
  - Her poetry exhibits great intelligence and often evokes the agonizing paradox of the limits of human consciousness trapped in time.
  - Excellent sense of humor - range of subjects + treatment is amazingly wide.
  - 1775 poems continue to intrigue critics, who often express disagreement
    - Mystical, sensitivity to nature, exotic appeal
    - "a cat came at us speaking English"
