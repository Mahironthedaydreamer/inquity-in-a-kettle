- ###  Biography & Background
  - Born in Oak Park, Illinois, 1899
  - A central figure in early 20th-century American modernism
  - Experiences as journalist, soldier, and expatriate shaped his themes and style. Journalism shaped his “tight, spare” literary style
  - Reflected post–World War I disillusionment and cultural change
  - Reporter for The Kansas City Star—learned concise, direct prose

- ### Early Life & Influences
  - Loved nature and outdoor life—learned from his father
  - Influenced by his mother’s artistic background
  - Started writing for his school newspaper—developed journalistic precision
  - Rejected conservative values, sought adventure and independence
  - Writing Style & Techniques
  - Iceberg Theory: deeper meaning hidden beneath surface details
  - Minimalist prose: short sentences, simple diction, emotional restraint
  - Objective tone: detached yet powerful storytelling
  - Created depth through implication, subtext, and dialogue

- ### Major Themes
  - War, love, loss, courage, masculinity, and existential struggle
  - Explores human endurance and moral strength amid chaos
  - Reflects postwar alienation and search for meaning

- ### Major Works
  - The Sun Also Rises (1926)  
    - Hemingway’s breakthrough novel about the “Lost Generation” after WWI
    - Follows Jake Barnes, a wounded veteran seeking meaning and identity
    - Themes: masculinity, disillusionment, aimlessness
  - A Farewell to Arms (1929)
    - Semi-autobiographical novel inspired by Hemingway’s WWI experiences
    - Story of an American ambulance driver and an English nurse’s tragic love
    - Themes: war’s futility, love, loss, and disillusionment
    - Noted for realistic tone, understated prose, and emotional restraint
  - For Whom the Bell Tolls (1940)
    - Set during the Spanish Civil War, inspired by Hemingway’s reporting
    - Themes: duty, sacrifice, heroism, and moral struggle
    - Reflects Hemingway’s political engagement and anti-fascist ideals

- ### Influence & Legacy
  - Transformed 20th-century prose and the modern short story
  - Inspired “New Journalism” and minimalist fiction
  - Continues to shape modern writers and storytelling worldwide
