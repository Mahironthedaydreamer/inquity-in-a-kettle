Major American Modernist writer – “Voice of the Jazz Age”

- Life & Literary Career
  - Born in St. Paul, Minnesota, into an upper-middle-class family.
  - Family’s financial instability shaped his view of wealth and class.
  - Educated at Princeton; served in the army during WWI.
  - Early exposure to social contrasts influenced his later themes
  - Rose to fame in the 1920s as the chronicler of the Jazz Age.
  - Explored materialism, love, and the American Dream.
  - Works reflected both the glamour and disillusionment of his era.

- Themes
  - American Dream: pursuit of wealth and social status → disillusionment.
  - Materialism & Moral Decay: critique of 1920s excesses.
  - Class & Identity: conflict between “old money” and “new money.”
  - Search for Meaning: characters caught between success and emptiness.

- Writing Style
  - Lyrical, poetic prose with vivid imagery and rhythm.
  - Elegant, musical language balanced with emotional depth.
  - Non-linear structure, unreliable narrators, flashbacks.
  - Blended poetic description with sharp social observation.

- Major Works
  - The Great Gatsby (1925): critique of the American Dream.
  - Tender Is the Night (1934): explores wealth, fame, and decline.
  - This Side of Paradise (1920): his breakout novel.
  - The Beautiful and Damned (1922): moral decay and ambition

- Cultural Impact & Legacy
  - Defined the image of the Roaring Twenties in literature.
  - Inspired generations of writers with his lyrical style.
  - Helped shape the concept of the “Great American Novel.”
  - Works remain classics in American literature and education.
