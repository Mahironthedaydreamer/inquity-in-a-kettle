## Key Biographical Details

- Birth: Born Charles John Huffam Dickens on February 7, 1812, in Landport, Portsmouth.
- Childhood Struggle:
  - Received little formal education.
  - Forced to work in a shoe blacking factory after his father, mother, and most siblings were sent to the Marshalsea debtors’ prison.
- Influence: childhood poverty + feeling of abandonment influence his later views on social reform and his fiction.

## Early Career and Literary Breakthrough

- Education/Early Work:
  - Returned briefly to school after his father received an inheritance.
  - At 15, he became a clerk, then a shorthand reporter in the law courts, and finally, a parliamentary and newspaper reporter.
  - This period gave him an affection for journalism and contempt for the law and Parliament.
- First Publications: Began contributing stories and essays in 1833, later reprinted as _Sketches by “Boz”_ (1836).
- Fame:
  - Seven weeks after being invited to provide a comic serial to accompany engravings, the first installment of _Pickwick Papers_ appeared.
  - It was an instant success, making Dickens the most popular author of the day.
- Novels:
  - Went on to write 15 major novels (e.g., _Nicholas Nickleby_, _David Copperfield_) and countless short stories. _David Copperfield_ is noted as his most autobiographical book.
- Christmas: He is credited with inventing the modern Christmas literary genre, most famously with _A Christmas Carol_ (1843).

## Personal Life and Influence

- Marriage:
  - Married Catherine Hogarth in April 1836, with whom he had ten children.
  - The initially happy marriage ended in a scandalous legal separation, partly due to his involvement with actress Ellen Ternan.
- Diverse Careers:
  - Throughout his life, he was a journalist, critic, actor/stage manager, playwright, and philanthropist (supporting charities like schools for poor children).
- Cultural Impact:
  - His writing empathized with the poor and criticized the cruel and selfish. The term "Dickensian" still invokes certain societal conditions.
  - He created some of literature's most enduring characters, rivaling Shakespeare.

## Later Life and Death

- Public Life:
  - A forerunner of the modern celebrity author, he traveled extensively and spent his last years delivering wildly popular dramatic readings of his works.
- Death and Burial:
  - He died of a stroke at age 58 in 1870 at his country home, Gads Hill. Despite his wish for a quiet burial, he was laid to rest in Poet’s Corner, Westminster Abbey.
