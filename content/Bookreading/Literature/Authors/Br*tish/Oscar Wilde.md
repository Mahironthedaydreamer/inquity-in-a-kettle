- Background + History
  - Born on October 16, 1854, Dublin
  - Child of unconventional parents
    - Mother was a poet and journalist
    - Father was an Irish antiquarian, gifted writer and specialist in diseases of the eye and ear.
  - Studied at:
    - Portora Royal School, Enniskillen, Country Fermanagh (1864-71)
    - Trinity College, Dublin (1874-74)
    - Magdalen College, Oxford (1874-78)
    - 1878, received his B.A and moved to London
  - Tried for charges of homosexuality because of his relationship with Alfred Douglas
  - Died penniless in a Paris hotel at the age of 46, November 30 1900

- Writing style + Works
  - Wilde's youth coincided with the increasing crisis of bourgeois culture + hayday of [[Aestheticism]]
    - Vulgarity  of bourgeois life + money-making fever + hypocritical approach to moral standards -> Turned to the movement.
    - Lifestyle + humorous wit soon made him the spokesman for Aestheticism
  - Career:
    - Art reviewer(1881)
    - Lecturer in the US and Canada (1882)
    - Lived in Paris(1883)
    - Lecturer in Britain(1883-1884)
    - Regular contributor for Pall Mall Gazette and Dramatic View (mid-1880s)
    - Edited in 1887-1889 Woman's World
    - Published [[The Happy Prince and Other Tales]] (1888) for his two sons
      - [[The Nightingale and the Rose]]
    - Met Lord Alfred Douglas - the love of his life and his downfall
  - Reputation known between the years 1892 and 1895 with a series of highly popular plays
    - **Lady Windermere's Fan**
    - **A woman of No Importance**
    - **An Ideal Husband**
    - **The Importance of Being Earnest**
  - Before theatrical success, he made several essays
    - **The Decay of Lying**
    - **The Critic as Artist**
  - During his time in prison:
    - **De Profundis**
    - After release: **The Ballad of Reading Gaol**
  - His works + world outlook reflected within are often very contradictory
    - His tales were usually much deeper in their approach to the problem of Good - Evil than most decadent literature
    - Great emphasis on good qualities of the poor, and the vices of those who had power and money
      - Usually tragic endings - Good cannot triumph in a world full of Evil.
    - Not to say there aren't positive sides:
      - Great gift as a writer lies in the ability to express contradictions of life in accurate paradoxes - affirming seemingly absurd things
      - Paired with his outstanding knowledge of language + gift for dialogue -> sparkling with wit
      - Masterly approach to the choice of words + classic simplicity have placed him amongst the great writers of his country.
